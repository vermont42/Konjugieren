# G14 · second opinion · final verdict: refuted · final grade: none

Essay line 187, English: "all the way to that primordial cloud of supernova-enriched gas from
which the Solar System was born."

German counterpart, `docs/verb_history_de.txt` line 153: "bis hin zu jener Urwolke aus
supernova-angereichertem Gas, aus der das Sonnensystem geboren wurde."

The skeptic's kill survives my attack. I confirm it at grade **none**, and I reached that
conclusion by a different route than the skeptic did, because one of its four arguments is
materially overstated and one is a listed failure mode. The two that carry the verdict hold, and
I add a fifth the skeptic missed that is stronger than anything either prior agent wrote.

## What the skeptic got right

**The two epithets are not the same epithet, and the difference is the whole case.** Phase 1's
entire basis was that Conjugar's cluster A adjudicated this phrase already, so no research was
needed. It did not. Patch P3 changed *"supernova-gifted elements"* at line 82. That phrase is
anaphoric: it points back at the element list in line 80, and that list ends by saying gold and
uranium were "forged instead in the crush of collapse, in the winds of dying giants, and, for the
heaviest, in the collision of two neutron stars." So "supernova-gifted elements" contradicted the
sentence immediately before it, in its own paragraph, about the very elements it named. That is a
real internal contradiction and P3 was right to remove it.

Line 187 has no such referent. It attributes nothing to supernovae except enrichment of the cloud,
which is a non-exclusive claim about one channel. "X made all of these things" and "X contributed
to this thing" are different propositions, and settling the first does not settle the second.
Phase 1 transferred a verdict across that gap without noticing the gap existed.

**Phase 1's "What is actually true" section does not reach its own conclusion.** Everything Phase 1
says about multi-channel enrichment is correct, and none of it makes "supernova-enriched" false.
Supernova enrichment is a subset of multi-channel enrichment, not a rival to it. The skeptic named
this non sequitur exactly.

**The proposed replacement is worse.** "Star-seeded gas" sits one clause before "forged in dying
stars," so the paragraph would say "star" twice in ten words, and it buys no accuracy: a
neutron-star merger is a collision of stellar remnants, which "star-seeded" covers no better than
what it replaces. It also costs a German re-translation. I agree the trade is bad.

## Where the skeptic is wrong, or is not

**Its first argument is one of the failure modes I was sent to look for, but it is not
load-bearing.** "Phase 1 did no research, so nothing constrains the verdict" conflates a weak
source with a wrong claim. Those are different propositions and only the second decides a verdict.
The skeptic hedged this by labeling its three arguments "in ascending order of importance," and it
then did its own research and engaged the substance, so the bad argument sits at the bottom of the
pile doing no work. Noted, not fatal.

**Its third argument is overstated and I would not repeat it.** The skeptic wrote that the presolar
nebula's distinctness "above all" rests on 60Fe, that 60Fe "traces specifically to nearby
core-collapse supernovae," and that a meteoriticist "would defend [supernova-enriched] without
qualification." Each step is shakier than stated, and the skeptic's own headline source cuts
against it. Gounelle and Meynet 2012 is the paper arguing that 26Al, the short-lived radionuclide
that most distinguishes the early Solar System, came from the *wind* of a massive star rather than
from a supernova; its 60Fe is assigned to supernovae of an earlier generation on giant-molecular-cloud
scale, which is ordinary galactic inheritance rather than a distinctive local signature. Tang and
Dauphas then measured the initial 60Fe/56Fe ratio as low and uniform, and concluded that supernova
injection into the solar system or its parental cloud "is therefore not necessary" to account for
it. A meteoriticist working on short-lived radionuclides would qualify, not decline to qualify.

That correction does not rescue the finding. It concerns whether a *nearby, triggering* supernova
was needed, which is a different question from whether supernovae enriched the gas. On the second
question the literature is not close, and the skeptic's fourth point, the one it made almost in
passing, is the one that decides the case.

**The claimed internal disagreement does not merely fail to mislead. It does not exist, and the
essay's own arithmetic is why.** The skeptic argued rhetorically: openings enumerate, perorations
compress. That is true but soft, and it concedes more than it needs to. The harder point is that
line 80 does not distribute the enrichment evenly among channels. It reads "some shed quietly on
the winds of aging giants, **the rest** blasted outward by supernovae." By the sentence's own
grammar supernovae get the larger share, and the element list that follows assigns most of the
named elements to "the crush of collapse," which is a core-collapse supernova. The closing
therefore compresses to the channel the opening had already made dominant. Line 187 is a faithful
summary of line 80, not a competitor to it, and Phase 0's inventory note that "the two now
disagree" is wrong about the text it is describing. Phase 0 wrote that note as a to-do flag while
patching a different line under a scope restriction, which is exactly the circumstance in which
such a note gets written fast.

I also checked the one route by which this could still have been in scope as a *shape* of finding.
The Phase 1 prompt does direct researchers to check "the closing summary against the sections it
summarizes," so an internal-consistency finding is a legitimate thing to raise here. G14 raised the
right kind of question. The answer is simply that the closing summarizes the opening correctly.

## My own research

Five operations, three searches and two fetches, taking neither prior agent's sources on trust.

1. **Is "supernova-enriched" a description specialists apply to this cloud?** Yes, in titles, which
   is the strongest form of the evidence. Peer-reviewed work on the presolar cloud is published
   under "Chemical enrichment of the pre-solar cloud by supernova dust grains," "Evidence for
   nucleosynthetic enrichment of the protosolar molecular cloud core by multiple supernova events,"
   and "The Supernova Triggered Formation and Enrichment of Our Solar System." A phrase that
   specialists put in their own titles is not a phrase a specialist quibbles at, and "nitpick" is
   defined in this project as a specialist's quibble.

2. **Does that literature treat supernovae as the only enricher, such that the essay's epithet
   would over-claim?** No, and the same paper says both things without strain: the protosolar cloud
   core records "contamination ... by diverse supernova sources," and it also preserves AGB-outflow
   grains from "an older, galactically-inherited ... component." That is precisely the shape of the
   claim at line 187: true, partial, non-exclusive.

3. **By mass, do supernovae dominate the metals in gas like this?** Yes. The 2025 review chapter on
   nucleosynthesis and galactic chemical enrichment states that most of the metal mass forms in
   massive stars that end as core-collapse supernovae; alpha elements including oxygen, the most
   abundant metal by mass, are "mainly produced by core-collapse supernovae"; iron-peak elements
   are "largely from Type Ia supernovae"; AGB stars supply carbon, nitrogen and the main s-process
   component, with contributions from sodium through iron described as negligible. If a writer must
   compress four channels to one word, the yields tables pick the same word the essay picked.

4. **The strongest case against, tested directly.** I went looking for the specialist objection and
   found the real one: the short-lived-radionuclide story has moved away from a nearby supernova
   (Gounelle and Meynet's Wolf-Rayet wind for 26Al; Tang and Dauphas's low, uniform 60Fe). But the
   essay makes no triggering claim, no nearby-supernova claim, and no radionuclide claim. It says
   the gas was enriched by supernovae, which that same literature affirms while relocating the
   26Al. The objection lands on the skeptic's reasoning, not on the essay's sentence.

## Final disposition, and what Phase 4 should do

**Confirm the kill. Grade none. Take no action on line 187 in either language, and none on the
German at `docs/verb_history_de.txt` line 153.**

Phase 4 should also **not** carry the skeptic's 60Fe argument into any writeup or changelog. It is
the weakest thing in the G14 record and it is out of date in emphasis. If a one-line rationale is
wanted for why the row closed, use this one: the opening at line 80 already gives supernovae the
larger share of the enrichment, so the closing names the channel the essay itself made dominant,
and every source consulted treats "supernova-enriched" as a true and standard description of the
presolar cloud that does not exclude the winds and mergers the opening also names.

One residual, recorded so it is not rediscovered as a finding: if Josh ever wants the opening and
the closing to reach for the same word, that is a stylistic preference about a peroration, not a
correction, and it costs a German re-translation to buy nothing. It should not come back as a row.
