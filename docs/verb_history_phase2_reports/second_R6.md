# R6 · final verdict: partly · final grade: factual-error

Third-pass ruling on essay line 117 (`docs/verb_history.txt`) and its German counterpart line 83
(`docs/verb_history_de.txt`):

> 🐑 ~lengthened o-grade~: *ō (as in *n̥-péh₂-tōr)

Phase 1 graded this factual-error. The Phase 2 skeptic returned partly / nitpick. **I restore the
factual-error grade** while keeping the skeptic's correction to Phase 1's account of where the form
came from, and I adopt the skeptic's replacement prose. The skeptic's verdict does not survive:
its downgrade rests on a provenance argument that cannot bear on severity, and on a claim about
where the bad segment sits that is the opposite of true.

## What the skeptic got right

Three things, and they are not small.

**The provenance discovery is real, and I verified it independently.** Phase 1 wrote that
`*n̥-péh₂-tōr` "is not a reconstruction found in the reference literature" and that "the likeliest
explanation is a Proto-Indo-Europeanization of Greek ἀπάτωρ" by the essayist. The skeptic said the
string is printed verbatim on English Wikipedia's "Indo-European ablaut" page. It is. I pulled the
raw wikitext through `Special:Export` rather than the rendered page, so that template markup and
combining diacritics could not be smoothed over by a renderer, and the PIE column reads
`{{lang|ine-x-proto|n̥-péh₂-'''tōr'''}}`, with `n̥-péh₂-'''tor'''-m̥` on the o-grade row above it.
So the essay copied; it did not invent. Phase 1's diagnosis is wrong on attribution.

**The provenance is in fact broader than the skeptic claimed.** Bullet four is from the same page
too. The essay writes `*mḗh₁-n̥s`, with that hyphen, and Wikipedia writes `mḗh₁-n̥s` with that
hyphen. Both of the essay's lengthened-grade examples come from that one article.

**Its prose revision beat Phase 1's.** Phase 1 wrote "from the same root," which sits directly under
a bullet whose example is from a different root, so the nearest antecedent is the wrong one. Naming
`*bʰer-` fixes it. I adopt the skeptic's line unchanged.

I also credit the one genuine severity argument in the report: the proposition the bullet asserts,
that the lengthened o-grade is `*ō`, is true, and the `*ō` really is there in `-tōr`. That is why
this is a low-consequence factual error rather than a serious one. It is not why it stops being one.

## Where the skeptic is wrong, or is not

**The downgrade is a blame argument wearing a severity argument's clothes.** The skeptic's stated
reason for nitpick is that the false belief "is a belief the reader would equally have got from the
encyclopedia article the essay followed," so the essay "is wrong only in the way the standard
encyclopedia treatment it followed is wrong." Both sentences are about the essayist's culpability.
Neither is about the text. A fact-check asks whether the shipped app tells the reader something
false, not whether Josh is at fault for the falsehood. Every claim in this essay came from
somewhere; if inherited-from-a-tertiary-source were a mitigating factor, it would discount most of
the 111 inventoried claims at once, which is a reductio. The provenance finding earns its place by
correcting Phase 1's diagnosis. It does not touch the grade.

**And the source is not a "standard treatment." It is a typo, and its own table proves it.** This is
the point neither prior agent reached. Look at all five rows of Wikipedia's PIE column together:

| grade | PIE column | root shape |
|---|---|---|
| e-grade | `*ph₂-tér-m̥` | `*ph₂-` |
| lengthened e-grade | `*ph₂-tḗr` | `*ph₂-` |
| zero-grade | `*ph₂-tr-és` | `*ph₂-` |
| o-grade | `*n̥-péh₂-tor-m̥` | `*péh₂-` |
| lengthened o-grade | `*n̥-péh₂-tōr` | `*péh₂-` |

One table, one word, two irreconcilable shapes for its root. No analysis of "father" can have the
root be `*ph₂-` in three rows and `*péh₂-` in the next two. Whoever compiled the compound rows
retrojected the Greek recessive accent of ἀ-πά-τωρ onto the PIE and, having put an acute somewhere,
supplied the full-grade `*e` that an acute normally implies. That is a mechanical slip contradicted
three lines above it on the same page. So the skeptic's mitigation deflates on its own terms: the
essay did not follow a scholarly tradition that happens to be wrong, it copied one cell of a table
whose other cells disagree with that cell.

**The central claim about where the bad segment sits is backwards.** The skeptic's answer to its own
strongest counterargument is that "the spurious `*e` sits in a syllable the bullet neither points at
nor glosses," an "unattended" part of an ornamental form. Read the list as a list and the opposite
is true:

```
🐐 ~lengthened e-grade~: *ē (as in *mḗh₁-n̥s)
🐑 ~lengthened o-grade~: *ō (as in *n̥-péh₂-tōr)
```

In bullet four the acute and the macron sit on the **same vowel**: `ḗ` is the graded vowel, and the
diacritics agree on where to look. In bullet five they come apart. The macron is on `ō`, the acute
is on `é`, and the acute is the more conspicuous of the two. A reader who has just learned from
bullet four that the marked vowel is the one under discussion looks at bullet five and lands on `é`,
which is precisely the segment that cannot exist. Bullets one through three compound this: all three
put the graded vowel in the root of `*bʰer-`, training the reader to read the root slot. Bullet five
puts a spurious full grade in that slot and hides the real `*ō` in a suffix. The error is not in an
unattended syllable. It is in the syllable the notation marks and the list has taught the reader to
attend to.

This also disposes of the skeptic's reply to Phase 1's secondary point. Phase 1 objected that "the
accent mark sits on the root, so the bullet points at a syllable the notation does not stress." The
skeptic answered that "hysterokinetic and amphikinetic nominatives with retracted accent and
lengthened suffix are a real type." True, and beside the point: Phase 1's objection was expository,
about what the example teaches, and the skeptic answered a different, linguistic objection about
whether the form-type exists. The bullet is not a paradigm entry, it is a definition-list
illustration, and it fails at that job whatever the typology permits.

**Where the skeptic is not wrong.** Its "correction, not an error report" instinct is right about
consequence, and I want Phase 4 to hear that. No German learner is misinformed about ablaut, about
lengthened grades, or about anything downstream. The form carries no gloss, so no false meaning
attaches to it. Nothing later in the essay leans on it. I am overturning the grade, not the sense of
proportion.

**One thing both agents got mildly wrong, which Phase 4 should not act on.** Phase 1 called bullet
four "defensible but awkward" because lengthened grades are often products of Szemerényi's and
Stang's laws, and the skeptic went further, calling that "the one a linguist would actually make"
and using it to argue Phase 1's severities were inverted. Both were arguing against the essay's own
source without checking it. Wikipedia raises the Szemerényi caveat and then offers `mḗh₁-n̥s`
specifically as its example of a **true** lengthened grade, in contrast to the ones the caveat
covers. Bullet four is the essay's best-sourced bullet, not its shakiest. There is a real scholarly
disagreement underneath, since Wiktionary derives `*mḗh₁n̥s` from `*méh₁n̥s-s` by exactly the
compensatory-lengthening mechanism, but that is a live dispute among reconstructions, not an error
in the essay. Do not fold it into R6.

## My own research

Six searches. I took neither prior agent's sources on trust, and I went for a handbook, since both
prior agents ended on tertiary-only and both said so.

I checked the source claim first, because the skeptic's whole downgrade rested on it, and I checked
it through `Special:Export` wikitext rather than the rendered article, so that the exact combining
characters were visible. Confirmed, and it turned up the bullet-four provenance and the
five-row internal contradiction as a bonus.

Then the linguistic substance, where **Ringe 2006, _From Proto-Indo-European to Proto-Germanic_
(Oxford), settles it and is the handbook neither prior agent reached.** Ringe reconstructs the
nominative as `*ph₂térs` → `*ph₂tér` by Szemerényi's Law. The root is `*ph₂-`, zero grade, in the
nominative that the essay's bullet is a compound of. There is no `*peh₂-` grade of "father" in the
paradigm at all.

The Greek quantity argument the skeptic rested on is independently sound, and I can now say why
without appealing to Wiktionary's accent marks. Greek α in this word is the reflex of a vocalized
`*h₂` with no preceding vowel, which is the standard demonstration case for laryngeal coloring:
PIE had no `*a`, so the `a` of πατήρ exists only because `*h₂` vocalized. A full-grade `*peh₂-`
would have given `*eh₂` > Attic-Ionic η, Doric long ᾱ, on the model of `*méh₂tēr` > μήτηρ / Doric
μᾱ́τηρ. πατήρ is not **πητήρ, and ἀπάτωρ is transparently the same stem behind an alpha privative.
So `*péh₂-` is excluded twice over, by the handbook paradigm and by the Greek reflex.

I confirmed the replacement independently: `*bʰṓr` "thief", a root noun of `*bʰer-` "to carry" from
earlier `*bʰórs`, continued by Greek φώρ and Latin fūr, is carried in both de Vaan's Latin and
Beekes's Greek etymological dictionaries in the Leiden series, not only on Wiktionary. My Perseus
fetch for the LSJ entry on ἀπάτωρ returned only the index scaffolding, no entry text; I did not
need it once Ringe was in hand.

## Final disposition, and what Phase 4 should do

**Restore Phase 1's grade of factual-error. Keep the skeptic's prose. Verdict partly, because Phase 1
was right about the form and the fix and wrong about the form's origin.**

The reason for factual-error over nitpick, stated for a reader who has seen neither prior report:
the grades in this system name kinds of defect, not priorities. "Needs-hedging" sits beside
"factual-error" as a sibling, and it is a kind, not a rank. Under that reading, nitpick means
"arguably not even wrong," and this is wrong. The essay prints, as bare fact and with no hedge, a
reconstructed word that cannot have existed, in the one passage where it defines the concept the
whole app is built around. That it is inconsequential makes it a small factual error. It does not
make it a matter of taste.

For Phase 4, concretely:

1. Replace `docs/verb_history.txt` line 117 with the English below.
2. Replace `docs/verb_history_de.txt` line 83 with the German below. Both localizations ship as
   "translated," so leaving the German is shipping the same false form to German readers.
3. Carry both into `Localizable.xcstrings`. The English gloss uses ASCII double quotes around
   "thief," matching bullet one's `"to carry"`, so the xcstrings edit changes ASCII `"` inside a
   JSON string value. Per CLAUDE.md that must go through Python on the raw file, not the Edit tool,
   and `git diff --stat` must show insertions with zero deletions.
4. This is a one-line substitution in each language. Do not rework the passage, and do not touch
   bullet four.

Markup check on both replacement lines: tildes balanced, one pair; asterisks unpaired as required;
no `$…$`, no `‡…‡`; the leading 🐑 is a bare bullet emoji, not `^…^`, matching bullets two through
five as they already stand. No new parenthetical aside is introduced. The existing parenthetical
slot gains one element and the line stays within a few characters of the original length.

One deliberate notation choice: I write `*bʰōr` with the macron alone, not the lemma spelling
`*bʰṓr` with a stacked acute. The essay never explains its accent notation, a monosyllable's accent
is uninformative, and the bullet's entire job is to show vowel length. Dropping the acute puts the
only diacritic on the only vowel that matters, which is exactly the property bullet four has and the
current bullet five lacks.

## Replacement prose

English, `docs/verb_history.txt` line 117:

> 🐑 ~lengthened o-grade~: *ō (as in *bʰōr, "thief", from *bʰer-)

German, `docs/verb_history_de.txt` line 83:

> 🐑 ~gedehnte o-Stufe~: *ō (wie in *bʰōr, "Dieb", von *bʰer-)

The German echoes the register of its own bullet one, which glosses `*bʰer-` as `"tragen"` in bare
quotes. Reconstructed forms are not localized, per the relocalization rules.
