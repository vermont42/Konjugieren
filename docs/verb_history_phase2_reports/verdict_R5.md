# R5 · verdict: refuted · grade: none

## Why

The sentence at line 110 reads:

> ~Voice~ distinguished active and middle (the latter indicating action affecting the subject or done in the subject's interest), with a developing passive.

Phase 1's complaint is that the coda "with a developing passive" reads as a third item in a list of
PIE categories and so invites the reader to think a passive was under construction in the parent
language. Two things defeat that.

First, the sentence does not put the passive in the list. The verb "distinguished" governs exactly
two items, active and middle. "With a developing passive" is a coda, and the participle "developing"
is itself the hedge: it says in one word that the passive was not a settled third voice. An essay
sentence that says a category was *developing* is not a sentence that says the category existed.

Second, and more decisively, "developing" is close to how the literature itself describes the
situation. The mainstream position is not that PIE was innocent of passive function and that the
daughters invented it from nothing. It is that the middle already carried passive function, that the
extension of the middle to passive use is placed at a late stage of PIE or just after, and that what
the daughters innovated independently is *dedicated passive morphology*, not passive meaning.
Kulikov and Lavidas (2013: 112), the very authors whose abstract Phase 1 quotes for the "no
specialized markers" point, write that "the existence of the passive pattern can be posited for PIE
verbal syntax in spite of the lack of the passive voice sensu stricto." Grestenberger (2018) goes
further and concludes that "(eventive) long and short passives can be reconstructed as a 'function'
(syntactic context) of non-active morphology in PIE," adding that "absence of dedicated passive
morphology does not mean that passive constructions cannot be expressed." A phrase whose content is
"a passive was on the way but was not yet its own thing" is a fair one-clause summary of that.

Phase 1's own framing concedes most of this: it grants that "there is a charitable reading, that the
middle was already doing passive work and so contained the seed, and that reading is true," and says
"I would not fight hard for it." Having granted the charitable reading is true, the residual claim is
only that a different reading is available. That is not enough to make an error, and this is an essay
for language learners, where a coda saying "a passive was developing" costs a reader nothing.

The German at `verb_history_de.txt` line 76 carries the identical hedge, "mit einem sich
entwickelnden Passiv," so there is no English-hedged, German-flat asymmetry to worry about.

No change is needed. Grade: none.

## What is actually true

PIE is reconstructed with a two-way voice opposition, active versus middle (or non-active). There is
no reconstructible dedicated passive morphology: apart from *-i̯(e/o)-, for which a passive function
cannot be reconstructed for the proto-language, there is no specialized passive marking. Passive
meaning is carried by middle inflection, and the specialized passive formations of the daughters, the
Greek -ē-/-thē- aorist and the Indo-Aryan -ya- presents among them, are independent post-breakup
innovations that are not cognate with one another.

Where Phase 1's account goes wrong is its last clause: "the passives of the daughter languages are
independent later creations rather than a shared inheritance in progress." That is true of the
*morphology* and false, or at least strongly contested, of the *function*. Kulikov and Lavidas posit
the passive pattern for PIE syntax despite the absence of a passive voice sensu stricto;
Grestenberger reconstructs both short and long eventive passives as syntactic contexts of PIE
non-active morphology, with demoted agents in the instrumental, and gives cognate "passive equations"
across Hittite, Tocharian, Vedic, and Greek (*demh₂, *ḱlew(-s-), *bʰer(-e-), and others). The passive
use of the middle is on that account precisely a shared inheritance. So "a shared inheritance in
progress" is nearer the truth than Phase 1's denial of it, and the essay's "developing passive" sits
on the right side of that line.

## Phase 1's replacement prose

> ~Voice~ distinguished active and middle (the latter indicating action affecting the subject or done
> in the subject's interest), and the middle carried the work a passive would later do.

The markup is balanced and the house style is respected, so it would not crash the Info screen. The
content is not false either. But it is not an improvement, and it trades one imprecision for another.
"A passive would later do" pushes all passive work into the daughters, which understates a PIE
passive *function* that Kulikov and Lavidas and Grestenberger both reconstruct; the original's
"developing" actually locates the passive in the parent, in progress, which is where the literature
puts it. It is also flabbier: it converts a five-word coda into a coordinated clause and repeats
"the middle," which the parenthesis has just glossed. Adopting it would make the sentence slightly
longer and slightly less accurate.

## Strongest case for the finding, and my answer

The strongest case is a genre argument rather than a factual one. The paragraph sits in a run of
parallel entries, "~Aspect~ was paramount," "~Tense~ was secondary," "~Mood~ was richly developed,"
each of which enumerates PIE categories, two of them with bulleted lists. A reader primed by that
rhythm may parse "with a developing passive" as a third bullet in disguise and come away thinking PIE
had an embryonic passive *form*, which is exactly what is not reconstructible.

My answer: the parse does not survive the word "developing," which is the one word in the clause
doing semantic work, and which no reader can take as asserting an existing category. And even the
misreading lands the reader somewhere defensible, because on the mainstream account a passive
function was in fact emerging in late PIE out of the middle. For the misreading to actively misinform
someone, they would have to infer a specific dedicated passive affix, and nothing in the sentence
offers one. Compare the sentence two paragraphs up, where the essay does flag a genuinely disputed
item explicitly ("whether it goes back to Proto-Indo-European at all is disputed"): the essay knows
how to hedge when a hedge is load-bearing, and here the hedge is already inside the adjective.
