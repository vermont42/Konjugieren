abbinden	gloss says 'untie, undo'; that sense exists regionally (ein Tier abbinden = losbinden), but the far more common everyday sense is 'tie off' (a wound), 'thicken' (a sauce), or 'set' (concrete). Sentence uses the tie-off sense.
abbleiben	gloss says 'be located', which looks like an import error; abbleiben is an archaic/regional variant of wegbleiben/ausbleiben ('stay away, fail to appear').
ablagern	gloss says 'deposit'; in everyday German ablagern is usually reflexive — a substance settles or is deposited somewhere
ablöschen	gloss 'extinguish, put out' fits the quenching sense shown here; in cooking ablöschen means to deglaze a pan with wine or stock
abregen	colloquial and reflexive: sich abregen — the gloss 'calm down' is right, but the verb needs a reflexive pronoun
abrollen	gloss says 'roll off'; the everyday transitive sense is to unroll or unwind something from a coil
absaufen	colloquial; besides 'drown', absaufen is the standard informal way to say an engine flooded out or a cellar went under water
abschinden	abschinden is reflexive (sich abschinden) in this 'slave away' sense
absenden	gloss says 'forward, send on'; absenden standardly means to dispatch or send off (e.g. a form or letter), not to forward something received
abtöten	gloss says 'mortify'; abtöten most commonly means to kill off or destroy (germs, cells, nerves) — the religious 'mortify the flesh' sense is rare
andienen	gloss says merely 'press'; andienen means to offer or press something on someone insistently, often reflexively (sich andienen, to make oneself available)
anhauen	gloss says 'hit'; in everyday German jemanden anhauen means to accost someone with a request (to hit someone up), not to strike them
anmalen	gloss says 'draw'; anmalen means to paint or color something in, not to draw (zeichnen)
anreisen	gloss says 'travel, take a trip'; anreisen specifically means to travel to and arrive at a destination (e.g. an event or hotel).
anscheinen	gloss says 'appear, seem'; that is the adverb 'anscheinend'. The transitive verb anscheinen means to shine upon (light falling on something).
ansinnen	gloss says 'designate'; ansinnen means to demand or expect something (usually something unreasonable) of someone.
antanzen	gloss says 'approach or arrive dancing'; in everyday German antanzen is colloquial for showing up somewhere, often uninvited or unwelcome.
antrinken	gloss says 'get drunk or tipsy'; antrinken is reflexive in practice — sich einen Rausch/Mut antrinken, i.e. drink oneself into a state
anwerfen	gloss says 'throw at'; the everyday meaning of anwerfen is to start up an engine or machine (the literal throwing sense survives mostly for plaster: Mörtel anwerfen)
aufknüpfen	gloss 'hang' is right for this sense; aufknüpfen also means to untie a knot (einen Knoten aufknüpfen)
aufrauchen	gloss says 'rise, appear'; the standard transitive aufrauchen means to smoke something to the end or use up one's supply of tobacco
aufrechnen	gloss says 'charge'; aufrechnen more precisely means to offset one claim against another
aufscheinen	Austrian usage: aufscheinen means to be listed or on record in a document or register
aufschießen	gloss says 'shoot open'; aufschießen standardly means to shoot up or grow rapidly
ausdienen	gloss is right for the military sense; note that the far commoner everyday use is figurative — „der Toaster hat ausgedient" means the toaster has had its day
ausfolgen	gloss says 'follow, accompany'; ausfolgen is Austrian officialese meaning to hand over or release something to someone
auslagern	gloss 'swap' captures only the computing sense (paging memory out to disk); the everyday meaning is to outsource work or move goods into external storage
ausloben	gloss 'offer' is broader than the verb: ausloben means specifically to publicly offer a reward or prize
auspfeifen	gloss says 'express discontent'; auspfeifen means specifically to boo by whistling — in German stadiums a whistle is a jeer, not applause
ausrauchen	gloss 'smoke out' belongs to ausräuchern; ausrauchen means to finish smoking something, or — of a fire or volcano — to stop giving off smoke
ausschauen	ausschauen is the southern German / Austrian everyday equivalent of aussehen ('look, appear'); it can also mean to look out for something (nach etwas ausschauen).
ausschweigen	sich ausschweigen is reflexive and means specifically to keep silent about a particular topic, not simply to stop talking.
ausspeichern	Technical term from logistics and computing (the counterpart of einspeichern), meaning to draw goods or data out of storage.
aussteuern	This dowry sense is archaic; in modern German aussteuern more commonly means to adjust an audio/signal level, or to drop someone from insurance benefits.
austräumen	Usually appears as the past participle ausgeträumt in the idiom 'der Traum ist ausgeträumt' — the dream is finished/dead.
auswachsen	In this 'right itself' sense the verb is reflexive (sich auswachsen): a problem that resolves on its own as things grow or develop.
autostoppen	Fairly rare and colloquial; the more usual everyday word is trampen. Placed in a subordinate clause here so the separable verb reads naturally without splitting.
beehren	gloss says 'compliment'; beehren means to honor someone with one's presence or attention, and is often used ironically
beigehen	gloss only cross-references drangehen; the relevant sense is 'to set to work, get stuck in', not the telephone sense of drangehen
bescheißen	gloss gives the literal 'shit on, sully with feces'; in everyday German bescheißen is a crude term for 'to cheat, swindle, rip off', which is the sense shown here.
beschlafen	gloss 'sleep with' is the sense shown; note that colloquial German more often uses beschlafen figuratively for 'to sleep on a decision' (eine Sache beschlafen).
bewenden	gloss says 'put to rest'; in modern German bewenden survives only in the fixed idiom „es dabei bewenden lassen“ (to leave it at that), never as a freely used verb
draufgehen	gloss says 'fall apart'; colloquial draufgehen most commonly means to be used up (money, time) or to perish
durchbrennen	gloss says 'continue to burn'; durchbrennen standardly means a bulb or fuse burns out (and colloquially, to elope)
durchgeben	the gloss's literal sense is real, but the far more common use of durchgeben is to relay information: „die Ergebnisse durchgeben“
durchgreifen	gloss says 'grasp through'; that literal reach-through sense exists but is rare — everyday durchgreifen means to take decisive action, to crack down
durchschwimmen	gloss says only 'swim'; separable dúrchschwimmen means to swim through or without stopping, while inseparable durchschwímmen means to swim across something („den Ärmelkanal durchschwimmen“)
einjagen	gloss says 'fill with'; einjagen only occurs in the idiom jemandem Angst/einen Schrecken einjagen — to instill fear or give someone a fright.
einkriegen	gloss says 'catch up'; in modern standard German einkriegen occurs almost only reflexively as „sich nicht einkriegen können" — to be unable to compose oneself, usually from laughter or amazement
entgegensehen	gloss says 'look at'; entgegensehen (+ dative) standardly means to look forward to or await something
entmieten	gloss is a truncated 'of a landlord'; entmieten means for a landlord to empty a building of its tenants, typically to renovate or sell
erstinken	The literal sense "become stinky" is what the sentence shows, but in living German the verb survives almost only in the fixed pair erstunken und erlogen ("a pack of lies").
erwünschen	erwünschen is chiefly reflexive (sich etwas erwünschen) and rarer than the plain sich wünschen, but does mean to wish/desire for oneself.
fernschauen	gloss says 'look into the distance'; fernschauen is the Austrian/Southern German word for watching television, exactly like fernsehen.
freihaben	gloss says 'be on vacation'; freihaben means to have time off from work or school generally, not specifically to be on vacation
gehaben	gloss says 'behave'; in living German gehaben survives almost only reflexively in the archaic farewell „gehab dich wohl“ / „gehabt euch wohl“
glattstellen	gloss says 'balance'; glattstellen is a finance term meaning to close out or square an open position
gleichschalten	gloss says 'synchronise'; the technical sense is obsolete and gleichschalten now almost always means forcing institutions into ideological conformity, with strong Nazi-era connotations
heimleuchten	Sentence follows the literal gloss (to light someone's way home); note that „jemandem heimleuchten" is also idiomatic for telling someone off.
herausstreichen	gloss says 'highlight, underline'; herausstreichen means to emphasize or single out for praise in speech or writing, not to mark text visually (it can also mean to strike a passage out)
hereinlegen	gloss says 'lay down inside, put'; the dominant everyday sense of hereinlegen is to trick, dupe, or take someone in — the literal 'lay something inside' reading is marginal
herumhängen	gloss covers only the literal sense; colloquially herumhängen far more often means to hang around or loaf about (Er hängt den ganzen Tag vor dem Fernseher herum)
herumschlagen	gloss says 'struggle, argue, deal with'; herumschlagen is almost always reflexive — sich mit etwas herumschlagen = to grapple with something
herumspinnen	gloss says 'spin around'; colloquially herumspinnen means to act crazy, mess around, or talk nonsense (also 'go haywire' of a machine)
herumsprechen	gloss says 'travel, get around, get out'; the verb is reflexive — sich herumsprechen = (of news) to get around
hinauslehnen	gloss says 'lean out'; the verb is reflexive — sich hinauslehnen = to lean oneself out
hinstürzen	the gloss's 'rush somewhere' sense is real, but in everyday German hinstürzen far more often means to fall down hard, to go sprawling
hinwegsetzen	gloss says 'leap over, jump over'; that literal sense is rare today, and sich über etwas hinwegsetzen standardly means to disregard or flout something
kaltmachen	gloss 'off someone' is right: kaltmachen is slang for killing someone
krankmachen	gloss 'make sick' is right for this transitive use; colloquially krankmachen is also used intransitively for skiving off work like krankfeiern
kürzertreten	gloss says 'economize'; kürzertreten more broadly means to scale back or take it easier, most often with workload or commitments rather than money
langmachen	gloss 'stretch' fits, but the verb is essentially reflexive: sich langmachen, to stretch oneself out or up
lockermachen	gloss says 'loosen'; written as one word, lockermachen is chiefly the colloquial 'cough up money'. The literal sense is spelled locker machen.
losbrechen	gloss 'break off' covers the rarer transitive use (einen Ast losbrechen); the everyday sense is intransitive: a storm, panic, or cheering breaks out
losschießen	the gloss sense is correct; in everyday speech the imperative schieß los! far more often means 'out with it, fire away'
mitzeichnen	gloss says 'draw along with others'; in everyday (esp. administrative) German mitzeichnen far more commonly means to countersign/co-approve a document (Mitzeichnung).
nachbacken	gloss says 'recook'; nachbacken specifically means to bake something in imitation of a recipe or model, not to reheat/cook again.
nachleben	gloss says 'follow'; nachleben means to live in accordance with an example or set of principles, not to follow physically
nachsingen	gloss says 'repeat'; nachsingen specifically means to sing something back after hearing it, i.e. to imitate a sung line
nachsteigen	gloss says 'follow'; colloquially jemandem nachsteigen means to pursue someone romantically, not simply to walk behind them
niederführen	gloss says 'run someone over', which belongs to niederfahren; niederführen means to lead downward
niedermachen	gloss 'slaughter, massacre' is the literal sense used here; niedermachen is also common figuratively for tearing someone apart with harsh criticism
ranhalten	in the 'get a move on' sense ranhalten is reflexive (sich ranhalten), as used here
rauslassen	rauslassen generally means 'let out'; the 'drop off' gloss fits the sense of letting a passenger out of a car, as here
reinfallen	gloss 'fall into' is the literal sense used here; reinfallen also colloquially means to be fooled or taken in
reinhauen	gloss 'beat up' only fits the fixed phrase „jemandem eine reinhauen" (land someone a punch); the far more common everyday sense of reinhauen is to tuck into food („Hau rein!")
rübermachen	gloss is broadly right, but in practice rübermachen is used above all of crossing the inner-German border from East to West
schleißen	gloss says 'strip, wear away'; the everyday sense is the handcraft term 'Federn schleißen' (to strip the vane from goose feathers), used here.
sonderkündigen	Rare legal coinage; the separable finite form is awkward in practice, so the natural infinitival frame ('durfte ... sonderkündigen') is used to show it.
umbrechen	gloss 'wrap' is the typesetting sense (Zeilen umbrechen, to break/wrap lines); separable umbrechen also means to plow up a field
umlagern	gloss 'transfer, relocate' describes the separable úmlagern (to move goods to another storage place); the shard marks this verb inseparable, and inseparable umlágern means to surround, throng, or besiege
umschulen	gloss says 'move schools'; umschulen most commonly means occupational retraining, though it also covers transferring a child to another school
umtreten	gloss says 'boot'; umtreten means to knock something over by kicking or trampling (treten 'to kick' + separable um- 'over')
untermalen	gloss says 'underpaint'; in everyday use untermalen most commonly means to underscore or accompany (e.g. with background music), though it also names an underpainting layer
verbeißen	gloss says 'bite the bit'; verbeißen is normally reflexive — sich etwas verbeißen means to suppress a laugh or remark, and sich in etwas verbeißen means to sink one's teeth into something.
verbrechen	in this sense verbrechen is colloquial and jocular: 'to have perpetrated' some botched piece of work.
vergucken	vergucken is reflexive — sich vergucken means to see something wrong; colloquially it can also mean to fall for someone.
verklicken	reflexive (sich verklicken): to click the wrong thing by mistake, typically on a screen
verkochen	gloss 'vaporize, forwall' is garbled; verkochen means (of liquid) to boil away, or to overcook something
verlesen	in the 'misread' sense verlesen is reflexive (sich verlesen); non-reflexive verlesen means to read aloud
verrauchen	in this sense verrauchen means (of anger or emotion) to disappear, literally to 'go up in smoke'
verrennen	reflexive (sich verrennen): to go down the wrong track, to become stubbornly fixated on a mistaken course
verstehlen	gloss says 'steal'; sich verstehlen is a literary, reflexive verb meaning to steal away, to slip off unnoticed, not to take something
versteuern	gloss says 'put a tax on something'; versteuern is what the taxpayer does — pay tax on one's own income. Imposing a tax is besteuern.
verstreben	gloss gives the noun 'strut'; verstreben means to brace or reinforce a structure with struts
vorbauen	gloss says 'preassemble'; vorbauen idiomatically means to take precautions / guard against something in advance (Vorsorge treffen)
vorbeischauen	gloss says 'look past'; vorbeischauen idiomatically means to drop by / stop in for a short visit
vorgreifen	gloss says 'reach forward'; vorgreifen idiomatically means to anticipate / preempt / jump ahead of something
vorkehren	gloss says 'put on display, show off'; that is hervorkehren (etwa den Chef hervorkehren). Plain vorkehren means to take measures or make provision — Vorkehrungen treffen.
weiterverschenken	same gloss ('regift') as weiterschenken; weiterverschenken is the more common everyday synonym.
widersagen	gloss says 'object, contradict' (that is widersprechen); widersagen standardly means to renounce or abjure, chiefly in religious formulas
zersingen	gloss says 'break through singing'; zersingen is a term from German folk-song scholarship for a song being altered and degraded by oral transmission
zudringen	gloss says 'approach, come closer'; in actual use zudringen takes a dative and means that a sound or a piece of news reaches someone
zupasskommen	gloss says 'be appropriate for'; zupasskommen is the fixed idiom 'zupass kommen', to come in handy or at the right moment
zurückwirken	gloss says 'have a retroactive effect'; zurückwirken normally means to have an effect back on something (feedback). The legal sense of 'retroactive' is usually rückwirkend/rückwirken.
zusammenkehren	gloss says 'pile up'; zusammenkehren means to sweep something together into a pile with a broom
zusammenräumen	gloss says 'pack up'; zusammenräumen ordinarily means to tidy up or clear away a mess
zusammenspielen	gloss gives only 'collude'; the everyday sense of zusammenspielen is to play together or interact, and the collusion reading is the secondary, pejorative one shown here
zustellen	gloss says 'block'; this is a real but secondary sense of zustellen (obstruct by piling things up) — its far more common meaning is 'deliver' (mail, parcels)
überdecken	authored for the separable über|decken ('lay a cover over', matching the shard's separability and gloss); note the inseparable überdecken means 'to cover up / mask'
übereinanderhalten	gloss says 'keep shut, clench'; übereinanderhalten literally means to hold one thing on top of another — authored for that transparent sense
überfordern	gloss says 'overcharge'; überfordern means to overtax / demand too much of someone. Overcharging a price is zu viel berechnen or übervorteilen, not überfordern
überhelfen	gloss says 'force on someone'; separable überhelfen standardly means to help someone put a garment on (parallel to überziehen)
überschießen	gloss says 'shoot something over something'; in everyday use separable überschießen means to shoot over or past the target
überzahlen	gloss says 'pay, transfer money', which describes überweisen; überzahlen means to pay too much for something
